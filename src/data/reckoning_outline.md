# The Reckoning Editorial Outline

## Epoch

**The Reckoning (2016–present)** — second Crisis phase of the Neoliberal Order. The last epoch,
and the only open one.

## Epoch identity

The Reckoning is the epoch in which the debts the order deferred come due, and the argument
is over who pays them. The Interregnum ended when a symptom announced itself as a
candidate. The Reckoning begins when he wins, and the question the Interregnum left open
(what happens when the symptom is given the office) is answered in public, and then answered again by his
successor, and again by his return. Four old bills arrive in this period: the social cost
of deindustrialization, the contradictions of a racial order older than the Constitution, a
legitimacy deficit in the institutions that was a generation in the making, and the
overextension of an empire that fought two wars it could not finish. A pandemic tests
the state, and the state's capacity is found uneven. A mob enters the Capitol. The Court
undoes a fifty-year settlement. A president returns to office with a program to remake
the executive branch from inside. The epoch is open. "Present" concedes that we are
inside the period and too close to see its shape.

## Historical thesis

The Interregnum produced a vacuum and the Reckoning is what filled it. **The vehicle** is a
candidate who won by repudiating the bipartisan consensus that had deferred the debts, against both parties'
establishments and against the polling. **The debts** arrive in sequence: the pandemic exposes
the state's capacity and its unequal protection, George Floyd's killing reopens the racial
question, the Capitol riot tests the transfer of power, Afghanistan ends the empire's
longest war, and *Dobbs* ends the Court's longest-standing settlement. **The institutions
are tested by the executive** rather than by crisis: the first impeachment, the
second, four criminal indictments, a Supreme Court decision on presidential immunity, and
then, in the second term, mass pardons of the January 6 defendants, the dismantling of
agencies by executive order, tariffs imposed by emergency declaration, military
deployments to American cities, and a war with Iran begun without a vote of Congress.
**The courts and Congress respond unevenly.** Some actions are blocked, some upheld,
some mooted, and the Court's own role in the system changes. **The question the epoch
cannot yet answer** is whether this is a new settlement or a longer crisis. Nothing in the
record yet says.

## Foundational question

> When the symptom is given the office, what debts come due, and who is asked to pay them?

This is the Interregnum handoff verbatim. The first half is the epoch's inventory and the
second half is the distribution question that has run through every Crisis epoch since
the Industrial Order's: who bears the cost of the order's failure.

Stress test: the pandemic, the Floyd protests, and Afghanistan ask which debts arrive and
whether the state can meet them. *Dobbs*, the immunity decision, and the second term's
use of the pardon, tariff, and deployment powers ask what the institutions do with an
executive who tests them. The 2016, 2020, and 2024 elections and January 6 ask whether
the transfer of power survives its own legitimacy crisis. It fits.

## Historical answer

Not yet, and that is the finding. The epoch's four old bills are all presented. The state
meets them unevenly, the loss falls on particular people (the unemployed of 2020, the
soldiers of Afghanistan, the women of states with abortion bans, the immigrants of the
second term), and the institutions that would settle the argument are themselves in it. The
epoch has not resolved into a new order.

## Big-picture story

Antonin Scalia died in February 2016 and the Senate refused to consider Barack Obama's
nominee. In November, Donald Trump won the Electoral College, 304 to 227, while losing the popular
vote by about 2.9 million. He signed a travel ban in his first week, fired the FBI director in
May 2017, and faced a special counsel for two years. He appointed three justices, signed
the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act in December 2017, and withdrew the country from the Paris
agreement and the Iran deal. Neo-Nazis marched in Charlottesville in August 2017. Democrats took the House in 2018.
The House impeached him in December 2019 for pressuring Ukraine, and the Senate acquitted him.

In March 2020 a pandemic closed the economy. Unemployment went from 3.5 percent in February to 14.7 percent in
April, Congress passed a $2.2 trillion rescue, and more than a million Americans died of
COVID-19 over three years. In May a Minneapolis police officer killed George Floyd, and
the protests that followed were among the largest in American history. Joe Biden won the
election in November. Trump contested the result, and on January 6, 2021, a crowd he
had addressed entered the Capitol while Congress counted the electoral votes. The House
impeached him a second time. In August 2021 the Taliban took Kabul as the last American
troops left, ending the twenty-year war.

Inflation, which had been dormant for forty years, reached 9.1 percent in June 2022. Russia
invaded Ukraine in February. In June the Supreme Court overruled *Roe v. Wade* in *Dobbs*. Trump was
indicted four times in 2023, convicted of felony falsification of business records in
May 2024, and shot at in Butler, Pennsylvania, in July. The Supreme Court held that
presidents have broad immunity from prosecution for official acts. Biden withdrew from
the race on July 21, and Trump defeated Kamala Harris in November, winning both the Electoral College and
the popular vote.

On January 20, 2025, Trump pardoned about 1,500 people charged in the January 6 attack,
signed 26 executive orders, and created the Department of Government Efficiency. What
followed, as reported (the 2025 and 2026 events are the least settled in this outline and need
verification): mass firings of inspectors general and federal workers, tariffs announced
April 2 under an emergency statute, a tax and spending law signed on July 4, National Guard
deployments to American cities, the longest shutdown in history in the fall of 2025, the
capture of Venezuela's president by American forces on January 3, 2026, the Supreme
Court's ruling on February 20, 2026 that the emergency statute did not authorize the tariffs, and a war with Iran begun
on February 28, 2026 by joint strikes with Israel, which has paused and resumed. The midterm
elections are on November 3, 2026.

## The edge decision

The Reckoning is the first epoch whose end has not happened. Three problems follow.

1. **There is no hindsight.** Events from 2025 and 2026 have no settled significance. The
   Iran War, the Venezuela operation, the 2026 shutdowns, and the tariff aftermath may matter
   more or less than they appear. Every earlier epoch was curated with a decade or more of
   distance.
2. **My knowledge of 2025 and 2026 is thin.** The events above come from web searches of
   Wikipedia timelines this session. I did not know several of them. Every 2025 and 2026 date is unverified and
   must be checked against primary sources before it goes into the instrument.
3. **The corpus will go stale.** The "present" edge moves, and the corpus will need updating.

**Proposed rule:** the corpus is written *as of* a stated date. I recommend October 19,
2026, two weeks before the midterms, and I recommend three guards:
(a) every 2025 and 2026 event carries a source URL and `date_verified: true`, since unlike
the earlier epochs these can be checked on the web today; (b) no event under twelve months old
goes to Level 1 unless it is structural beyond argument, which is why the Iran War is
provisional; and (c) the corpus header states the as-of date, and the instrument's chart
labels the final stretch as provisional. The alternative is to close the curated corpus at
December 31, 2025 and add 2026 as a thin Level 1 to 3 layer later.

  December 31, 2015. Scalia's death (February 13, 2016) and the Garland blockade are the first

- **The live COVID-19 span** (2020 to 2021) is made precise and replaced by label.
- **The live placeholders** for Trump elected, Charlottesville, January 6, and Trump re-elected

## What the viewer should understand

- The Reckoning is the period in which the order's deferred debts arrive, and it is
  defined by who is asked to pay them.
- The vehicle was a repudiation of the consensus, and the debts arrived regardless of who
  held office.
- The institutions, the courts, Congress, and the electorate, are in the argument, not above
  it.
- The period has no ending yet, and its last stretch is drawn as unsettled.
- The framework's claim is that this is a crisis phase of a cycle, not a new order, and the
  record so far does not contradict it or confirm it.

## Handoff

No successor exists, because the epoch is still open. In place of a handoff, the corpus
should close with the open question the framework cannot answer: *Whether the period that
began in 2016 resolves into a new settlement, or deepens into the kind of crisis that ends
the cycle, depends on events that have not happened. The instrument records the debts that
came due. It does not yet know who will pay them.*
