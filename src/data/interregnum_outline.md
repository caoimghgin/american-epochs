# The Interregnum Editorial Outline

## Epoch

**The Interregnum (2008–2015)** — first Crisis phase of the Neoliberal Order.

## Epoch identity

The Interregnum is the epoch in which the order's creed was dead and its machinery kept
running. The financial system failed in September 2008, the government saved it, and the
people who had borrowed against the order's promises were mostly left to carry the loss.
A new president arrived on a promise of change and governed inside the settlement he
inherited. Nothing replaced the order, and two movements appeared in the vacuum, one on the right and one on
the left, each naming the same betrayal and blaming a different party. The name is
Gramsci's: the old is dying and the new cannot be born, and in the interval a variety of morbid
symptoms appear. The epoch ends in June 2015, when a real-estate developer walked down an escalator and
announced he was running for president.

## Historical thesis

The End of History ended on its own premises before the crash named it. **The failure
is public and total:** Lehman Brothers collapses, the credit system seizes, and the
government writes the largest rescue in its history. **The rescue is the argument.**
The banks are saved on the order's terms, with no prosecutions and few changes in their
structure, and the homeowners who owed the money go to foreclosure. The order spends the
epoch discovering that its legitimacy rested on a promise, shared gains, that the rescue
revealed it could not keep. **The change that arrives is partial.** Obama's election is the
largest turn in the country's politics since 1992, and his administration stabilizes the
economy, expands health insurance through private markets, and leaves the financial structure and
the surveillance state in place. **The vacuum fills with symptoms.** The Tea Party
and Occupy are mirror images, one that blames government for helping the banks and one that blames the banks
for being helped. **Executive power and the courts continue their drift.** *Citizens United*
opens the political system to unlimited corporate spending, Snowden reveals the scale of
the surveillance state, and the party that wins the White House keeps the powers it
campaigned against. The epoch ends with the symptom that outlasts the rest.

## Foundational question

> When an order's legitimacy ends and nothing exists to replace it, who bears the loss and
> who fills the vacuum?

The End of History handoff asks exactly this. The first half is the distribution question,
and the answer is the Interregnum's whole story. The second half is the political
question, and its answer is not yet settled at the epoch's end.

Stress test: Lehman, TARP, and the foreclosure crisis ask who bears the loss. Obama's
election, the stimulus, and the Affordable Care Act ask whether the order can reform itself.
*Citizens United* and Snowden ask what the order does with money and power while it
decides. The Tea Party, Occupy, and Ferguson ask who fills the vacuum. It fits.

## Historical answer

The institutions were made whole and the households were not. The vacuum filled with
anger first and with a candidate afterward. The order's machinery, the banks, the Fed, the
security state, and the two parties, survived the crisis intact, and its legitimacy did not.

## Big-picture story

The housing boom turned in 2006 and the credit system froze in 2007. In March 2008 the
Fed engineered the sale of Bear Stearns to JPMorgan Chase. In September the government
took over Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy on the 15th, and
AIG was rescued the next day. Congress passed the $700 billion Troubled Asset Relief
Program on October 3, after the House rejected it once. Barack Obama won the presidency a
month later, the first Black president, with Democratic majorities in both houses.

His first two years produced the $787 billion stimulus, the auto rescues, a mortgage
program that helped few homeowners, the Dodd-Frank financial reform, and the Affordable
Care Act. The Supreme Court decided *Citizens United* in January 2010. The Tea Party
movement, which began as a protest against the bailouts, delivered the House to the
Republicans in November. Occupy Wall Street set up in Zuccotti Park in September 2011
and made "the 99 percent" a phrase. Obama killed Osama bin Laden in May 2011, withdrew the
last troops from Iraq that December, and was reelected in 2012 against an economy still at 7.9
percent unemployment. Edward Snowden's disclosures in June 2013 showed the surveillance
state the Patriot Act and the wiretapping program had built. In 2014, Eric Garner's death
and the killing of Michael Brown in Ferguson put policing at the center of the argument,
and ISIS took Mosul and declared a caliphate. In 2015 the Iran deal, Charleston,
*Obergefell*, and the Paris climate agreement crowded the summer, and on June 16 Donald Trump
announced he was running for president.

## Three-act structure

### Act I: The collapse and the rescue, 2008–2009

Bear Stearns, Fannie and Freddie, Lehman, AIG, TARP, the election, the auto rescue, the
stimulus, the mortgage program, the stress tests, and the end of the recession.

### Act II: The reform and the revolt, 2010–2012

*Citizens United*, the Affordable Care Act, Dodd-Frank, the Tea Party wave, the foreclosure
settlements, Occupy, the end of the Iraq War, bin Laden, the debt-ceiling standoff, and
Obama's reelection.

### Act III: The symptoms, 2013–2015

Snowden, the shutdown, the Affordable Care Act rollout, Ferguson, ISIS, Obergefell and
Charleston, the Iran deal, the Paris agreement, and Trump's announcement.

## Emotional arc

**Collapse → rescue → hope → revolt → disillusion → fracture → portent**

## Level 1 narrative spine

Nine point events plus the zero interest rate policy span (Snowden stays as the 2011 to 2015 pick, and the editor accepted the ZIRP span):

1. **Lehman Brothers fails** (September 15, 2008): the largest bankruptcy in history, and
   the moment the credit system seizes. The crash names what the End of History had
   already spent.
2. **TARP signed** (October 3, 2008): the $700 billion rescue of the financial system.
   The order's institutions are made whole, and the household loss is left to the
   households.
3. **Obama elected** (November 4, 2008): the first Black president, with unified
   Democratic government. The country votes for change, and the change is inside the
   settlement.
4. **Citizens United** (January 21, 2010): the Supreme Court holds that corporations and
   unions can spend without limit on elections. The order's money enters its politics
   without a limit.
5. **Affordable Care Act** (March 23, 2010): the epoch's one structural reform, built on private
   insurance and passed without a Republican vote. It becomes the most fought-over law of the
   decade.
6. **The Tea Party wave** (November 2, 2010): Republicans gain 63 House seats on a revolt
   against the bailouts and the health law. A symptom becomes a political force.
7. **Occupy Wall Street** (September 17, 2011): the left's mirror image, a protest against
   the bailouts' beneficiaries. It coins "the 99 percent" and then dissolves.
8. **Snowden's disclosures** (June 5, 2013): the surveillance state's scale is shown
   by one of its contractors. It continues the thread of executive power around the law
   from Iran-Contra through the Patriot Act and the wiretapping program.
9. **Trump announces** (June 16, 2015): a candidacy that the other symptoms did not
   produce and that ends the epoch.

**Spacing check:** the gaps between Level 1 points are about 0.05, 0.1, 1.2, 0.2, 0.6, 0.9,
1.7, 2.0, and a close of 0.55 years to the end of the epoch. The front is crowded: Lehman,
TARP, and the election fall within seven weeks, and *Citizens United* and the Affordable
Care Act fall two months apart. The back half is sparse, and the longest gap is the two years from Snowden's disclosures to Trump's announcement,
filled at Level 2 by Ferguson, ISIS, the Iran deal, Charleston, and *Obergefell*.

**Contested calls to argue about:**

- **Three events in seven weeks (Lehman, TARP, the election).** Each is a different
  claim: the failure, the rescue, and the change. TARP is the one to demote if the labels
  collide, but it carries who-got-preferred-treatment, which is the argument. I recommend
  keeping all three.
- ***Citizens United* at Level 1.** It is the rule-of-law and money-in-politics hinge, and
  the Court's second decision to reshape an election after *Bush v. Gore*. The alternative
  is Dodd-Frank, which is the financial reform. I put Dodd-Frank at Level 2 because it
  left the banks intact.
- **The Affordable Care Act at Level 1.** The epoch's rationale says "no structural reform,"
  and the ACA is the exception that is also a counter-example: it is a reform that preserves the
  order's market structure. It is the one thing the Democrats built, and its repeal fight
  defines the next decade.
- **Tea Party and Occupy both at Level 1.** The rationale calls them mirror-image symptoms,
  and they only mean something as a pair. Occupy was brief, and I have held it at Level 1
  for the argument and not for the duration.
- **Snowden vs the 2011 to 2015 alternatives.** The gap needs an event. Snowden continues
  your executive-power thread. The alternatives are Ferguson (policing, the other legitimacy
  crisis), *Shelby County v. Holder* (the Voting Rights Act gutted, June 2013), and Holder's
  "too big to jail" testimony (March 2013, the preferred-treatment thread). I would not
  argue hard against any of them. This one is yours.
- **Trump's announcement as the last Level 1.** It is a candidacy, not an act of state. The
  rationale makes it the boundary. The alternative closing events (Charleston, the Iran deal,
  *Obergefell*) are real but belong to a different story.
- **Obama's reelection (November 2012)** is Level 2. It ratified the settlement and changed
  nothing structural.
- **The Iraq withdrawal (December 18, 2011)** is Level 2 and carries the end of the Iraq
  War span. **Bin Laden (May 2, 2011)** is Level 2.
- **Dodd-Frank (July 21, 2010)** is Level 2, and the **foreclosure settlements and "too big
  to jail"** are Level 3. Promote them if you want the preferred-treatment thread louder.

## Long-running conditions (candidate spans)

- **The Iraq War (March 20, 2003 to December 18, 2011):** owned by the End of History corpus.
  This corpus carries its end events (the withdrawal at Level 2, ISIS and the return of advisers in
  2014 at Level 3).
- **The Afghanistan War (October 7, 2001 to August 30, 2021):** owned by the End of History
  corpus and runs through this epoch. The surge (December 2009), bin Laden's death, and the
  end of combat operations (December 28, 2014) are points here, and the Reckoning carries the end.
- **Zero interest rate policy (December 16, 2008 to December 16, 2015):** a candidate span, exactly seven years. It is the epoch's
  monetary signature: the Fed as the only institution willing to act, and asset prices as the
  result. It earns a lane by duration and by thesis, but it has no obvious popular event, and
  the lift-off in December 2015 is a closing beat. I recommend including it, and it's a close call.
- **The Great Recession (December 2007 to June 2009):** carried by the NBER overlay.
- **The foreclosure crisis (2007 to about 2012):** a condition without clean edges, carried by
  points (HAMP, robo-signing, the national mortgage settlement).
- **The Obama presidency (2009 to 2017):** already the president bars.
- **Threads (AE-16):** executive power and rule of law (the Guantánamo promise, drones, no
  torture prosecutions, Snowden), immigration (DACA, the Gang of Eight, DAPA, Trump's
  announcement), race and policing (Trayvon Martin, Ferguson, Charleston), and the war on
  drugs (the Fair Sentencing Act, marijuana legalization, the opioid epidemic).

## Boundary decisions

- **The start.** The corpus opens January 1, 2008 and follows the End of History's close on
  December 31, 2007. The first credit failures of 2007 are the previous epoch's last beats, and
  the Bear Stearns rescue (March 2008) is the first beat here.
- **The end.** The corpus closes December 31, 2015. The Reckoning opens on January 1, 2016.
  Trump's announcement (June 16, 2015) is the last Level 1 event here, and the Iowa caucuses
  (February 1, 2016), Scalia's death (February 13, 2016), and Trump's election (November 2016) belong to the Reckoning.
  The Fed's lift-off (December 16, 2015) is the last beat here.
- **The Iraq War span** was begun by the End of History corpus. This corpus carries the end
  events and will not carry a second span.
- **The Afghanistan War span** is owned by the End of History. This corpus carries its middle
  (the surge, bin Laden, the end of combat operations), and the Reckoning carries the end.
- **The live placeholders** for Lehman, the Tea Party wave, and Occupy are absorbed. The
  placeholders from 2016 onward are not touched.

## What the viewer should understand

- The crisis was the order's, and the rescue was the order's too.
- The financial system was saved on its own terms, and the question of who was saved
  is the epoch's argument.
- The change that came was real but partial, and it left the structures intact.
- The Tea Party and Occupy were two answers to one betrayal, and neither became a
  governing alternative in the epoch.
- The executive's expanded powers survived the change in party.
- The epoch ends with a symptom, not a settlement.

## Handoff

*By the summer of 2015 the order had survived its crisis and lost its argument. The banks
were rescued, the households were not, and a president elected on change had stabilized
what he found. Two movements had named the betrayal and neither had won. A candidate who
had been a symptom was running as a solution. The next question is what happens when the
symptom is given the office: what debts come due, and who is asked to pay them.*
